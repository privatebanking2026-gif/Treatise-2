PREFACE.

The only work on the law of set-off and mutual credit which has ever been presented to the profession (a summary published by Basil Montagu, Esq. in the year 1801), having never reached a second edition, the Author conceives this treatise will not be considered as entirely uncalled for.

It must be obvious to everyone who considers the nature of the war from which Great Britain has subsequently emerged, the extent of her commerce, and the important part she performed in the arduous struggle, that the intervening period cannot have elapsed without adding considerably to the number and variety of cases under this head of jurisprudence; arising out of the complicated interests of policy brokers, the assurers, and the assured.

Sect. 4.—Of the Mutuality of Debts and Credits.

To enable a party to avail himself of a set-off in a case of bankruptcy, the debts or credits must be mutual and due in the same right, as is the case with debts under the general statutes of set-off.
